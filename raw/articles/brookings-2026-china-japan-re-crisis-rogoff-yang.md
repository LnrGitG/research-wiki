# How long will China's real estate crisis last?

Brookings Podcast on Economic Activity, 16 April 2026  
Interviewees: Kenneth Rogoff (Harvard), Yuanchen Yang (IMF)  
Host: Gian Maria Milesi-Ferretti (Brookings)  
Full paper: "A tale of two countries: The real estate crises in 1990s Japan and contemporary China" (BPEA)

## Stylized facts — China

- RE + infrastructure = ~1/3 of GDP demand
- Investment/GDP = **40% for two decades** (epic by international standards; second only to Iceland pre-GFC)
- Household asset allocation: **70% in housing** (vs ~1/3 in US, Japan)
- Housing assets ≈ 5× GDP in China
- Crisis ongoing: **6th year of decline** (started ~2020)

## China vs Japan — parallels

| Dimension | Japan 1990s | China present |
|-----------|-------------|---------------|
| Overbuilding root cause | Yes (housing + "bridges to nowhere") | Yes — "brick by brick" |
| Banking crisis amplification | **Yes** (Bernanke channel) | **No/limited** — high savings, no lending culture |
| Wealth channel | Present | **First-order** (70% household assets in RE) |
| Local govt debt linkage | Present | Severe — land revenues for tier-3 cities |
| Unwinding duration | 10–20 years | Possibly longer — physical overbuild larger |

## Cross-sectional evidence (300 Chinese + 43 Japanese prefectures)

- Cities with largest cumulative construction during boom → deepest price declines in bust
- **Tier-3 cities** (millions of inhabitants, 60% of GDP): epicenter
- Population outflows, less prosperous, dependent on land sales for local revenues
- Pattern holds in Japan (excluding Tokyo/Osaka): more investment → lower prices

## Sentiment measurement (LLM-based)

**Method:** Large Language Models on newspapers/journals → measure real-time positive/negative housing sentiment. City-level disaggregation via local "attention to housing news" weighting.

**Identification:**
- Baseline wealth effect: ΔConsumption / ΔHousePrice ≈ **0.15** (β baseline)
- Sentiment amplification: ΔConsumption / (ΔHousePrice × Sentiment) = **~0.30** (2× baseline)

**Conclusion:** Sentiment channel doubles the baseline wealth effect.

## Amplification mechanisms (non-banking)

1. **Wealth collapse channel:** 70% of household assets in housing → massive perceived impoverishment, consumption cuts
2. **Local government debt channel:** land-sale revenues dried up → services cut → multiplier effect
3. **Sentiment channel (2× amplification):** LLM-measured negative sentiment multiplies price decline impact
4. **Physical overhang:** tier-3 cities have housing that nobody wants to occupy; cannot easily export demand away

## Key insight

Western RE crises usually viewed as **banking crises** (Bernanke 1983). China shows RE crises can be severe without credit overhang — via wealth, sentiment, and local-fiscal channels.

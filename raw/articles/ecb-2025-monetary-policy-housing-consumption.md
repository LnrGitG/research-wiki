# The ripple effects of monetary policy on housing and consumption

Niccolò Battistini, Johannes Gareis | ECB Blog, 31 July 2025

## Core question

How ECB interest rate changes transmit to housing affordability → housing sales → home goods consumption (furniture, appliances = ~6% of private consumption).

## Affordability Index construction

Mirrors US NAR Housing Affordability Index:

**Index = Nominal Household Income / Mortgage Payment**

For typical potential buyer:
- 25-year mortgage
- LTV 80%
- Current ECB mortgage rate (composite indicator)
- Transaction values linked to ECB quarterly HPI

**Dynamics Q1 2005 – Q1 2025:**
- 2008 (financial crisis) → LOWEST affordability
- End-2016 → PEAK affordability
- 2022 plunge: rate hikes outpaced income recovery
- Mid-2025: partial recovery, still below end-2021

## Structural Bayesian VAR (SBVAR)

**Variables in Cholesky order:**
1. Real GDP
2. GDP deflator
3. Home goods consumption
4. Housing sales
5. Nominal house prices
6. 3-month EURIBOR
7. Mortgage interest rates

**Estimation:**
- Q1 2006 – Q1 2025
- Pandemic heteroskedasticity adjustment (Lenza & Primiceri 2022)
- Cholesky identification
- Bachmann & Sims (2012) method for counterfactual with housing sales held constant

**Key finding:** Shock of +25bp → housing sales ↓2%, home goods consumption ↓0.3% after 3 years. Counterfactual (holding housing sales constant): consumption falls only 0.1%. **Housing sales channel amplifies consumption response by ~3x.**

## Empirical evolution since Q4 2021

- Home goods consumption fell ~10% Q4/2021 → Q4/2023
- Reduced total private consumption growth by ~0.6 percentage points
- Housedhold consumption = 6% of total → housing bust had outsized macro impact
- Stabilisation from Q2 2024; incipient recovery Q2 2025

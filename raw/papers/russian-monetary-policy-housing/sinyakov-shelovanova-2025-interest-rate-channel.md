# Demand for consumer loans in Russia: How strong is the interest rate channel of monetary policy?

**Авторы:** Andrey A. Sinyakov, Tatyana I. Shelovanova (Bank of Russia)  
**Журнал:** Russian Journal of Economics 11(1): 47-75, 2025  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.32609/j.ruje.11.145314

## Abstract

The booming retail trade and above-target consumer prices inflation in 2023–2024 in Russia, amid tightening monetary policy stance, raise an issue of the strength of the monetary policy interest rate channel. The focus of the paper is the interest rate elasticity (given inflation expectations) of a household's loan request probability.

## Key Findings

1. **Interest rate channel is weak for small changes:** A 1 percentage point increase in the interest rate lowers the loan request probability by only 1.5–2.3%.

2. **Strong effect for large changes:** A 10 p.p. rate increase from its average level reduces the probability by at least half its average level, potentially to zero.

3. **Policy implication:** This suggests that the Bank of Russia, relying on this channel for unsecured credit, might have to increase the key rate significantly to cool down consumer demand and bring retail inflation to the target.

4. **Inflation expectations matter:** Higher households' inflation expectations positively correlate with loan demand, thus households' inflation expectations have real effects. Anchoring inflation expectations is important for macroeconomic stability.

5. **Data:** Unique data from the All-Russian survey of consumer finances (6,000+ households), covering 2020–2022.

## Methodology

- Unit of analysis: household (not individual consumer)
- Dependent variable: probability of requesting a loan
- Key independent: interest rate (with inflation expectations control)
- Identification: uses interest rates from aggregator websites in regions of households' residence (exogenous variation)

## Relevance to Housing Market

Although the paper focuses on unsecured consumer loans, the findings have direct implications:
- If interest rate channel is weak for small changes, monetary policy may be less effective at cooling housing demand through modest rate hikes
- The "double function" of housing (basic need + investment) may amplify the effect of low rates (see also Smirnova 2025)
- Regional heterogeneity in monetary transmission (see Zvereva 2025, Demidova & Shchankina 2025) may affect housing markets differently

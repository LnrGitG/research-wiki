# Real Estate Market and Monetary Policy: Searching for Balance

**Автор:** Zhanna Smirnova  
**Источник:** Econs.online Opinions, July 18, 2025  
**Примечание:** Opinions published on Econs.online may not reflect the views of the editors and do not represent the position of the Bank of Russia.

## Key Arguments

### Macro-criticality of Real Estate

The real estate market is characterized by 'macro-criticality' — effects of changes in the market extend beyond it and may impact macroeconomic stability. Crises associated with housing market cause more severe drops in GDP and last longer than those caused by other reasons.

### Double Function of Housing

Housing performs two functions:
1. **Basic need** — structural demand influenced by demographic trends and condition of available housing
2. **Savings and investment vehicle** — tangible asset for savings and investment

When interest rates are low, **both functions are stimulated**:
- Households that planned to improve housing conditions rush to act
- Others see investment potential, buying at low rates for future resale or rental income
- **Result:** monetary policy transmission channels may work with **double the impact**

### Five Transmission Channels to Real Estate

1. **Interest rate channel** — direct impact on decisions to purchase housing and take mortgage loans. Low rates make it easier to choose between current consumption and future loan payments.

2. **Balance sheet channel** — changes in central bank rate trigger revaluation of collateral value, influencing loan demand. Rising housing prices increase collateral value, expanding credit capacity.

3. **Wealth channel** — works through income from assets. Rise in home prices boosts wealth of existing homeowners, expanding their ability to take loans (e.g., for additional property or upgrades).

4. **Risk-taking channel** — changes in monetary policy rates affect economic agents' risk perception and tolerance. Easing boosts appetite for risky investment; tightening dampens it. In real estate, low rates reduce likelihood of borrower default (job loss etc.), making institutions more willing to grant loans.

5. **Expectations channel** — central bank's words and actions affect economic activity by changing expectations of economic agents. Lower rates boost optimism about growth potential, encouraging expansion of activity until physical resources become limiting factor.

### "Irrational Exuberance" Risk

A drop in interest rates followed by rise in demand and inflow of funds to real estate may lead to disregarding physical resource constraints. At some point, opinion may prevail that demand will inevitably keep rising (partly under function of housing as basic need). This buyer behaviour is called **'irrational exuberance'** — demand in asset market pushes prices up, which in turn fuels demand even more, ultimately resulting in a bubble.

In this case, **expectations channel starts to prevail over risk-taking channel**, causing economic agents to underestimate risks. Borrowers undertake obligations without due regard for ability to meet them; credit institutions simplify audit standards and ease requirements.

### Macroprudential Policy as Complement

Monetary policy rate is comprehensive, not targeted instrument. To offset its impact in individual markets and prevent crises resulting from imbalances in real estate market, central banks introduced **macroprudential regulation** from 2008:
- Minimum down payment requirements for mortgages
- Debt ceiling for households applying for loans
- Macroprudential risk weight add-ons
- Limiting number of loans to individual types of borrowers

However, monetary policy and macroprudential regulation remain **complementary tools**.

### Policy Implication

The combination of transmission effects provides evidence that in the event of low interest rates, the impact of monetary policy on real estate market may be **stronger than needed** for economic activity to return to potential growth rates. This creates tension between:
- Need to stimulate economy through low rates
- Risk of overheating in real estate market and accumulation of financial stability risks

# Monetary Policy Transmission Mechanism: The Role of Household Heterogeneity and Spatial Effects

**Автор:** Valeria Zvereva (Bank of Russia; HSE University)  
**Журнал:** Russian Journal of Macroeconomics and Financial Economics (RJMF), 2025, Vol. 17, No. 3

## Abstract

The paper investigates the impact of regional economic inequality, including income and asset structure of the population, on the efficiency of the monetary policy transmission mechanism in Russia, namely, the interest rate and credit channels.

## Key Findings

1. **Regional inequality matters:** Monetary policy transmission is more effective in regions with:
   - High share of labour income in total income
   - Low share of transfers in income structure
   - Low share of poor population
   - High share of households able to purchase durable goods and build up financial assets

2. **Sectoral dependence:** Efficiency is higher in regions less dependent on agriculture and SMEs due to availability of subsidized lending programmes for these sectors.

3. **Spatial spillovers:** The impact of income inequality on monetary policy transmission in one region extends to its neighbours, which may be related to migration flows, resource shifts, as well as regions' sectoral specialisation.

4. **Robustness:** Despite the shocks of recent years and the structural adjustment of the economy, estimation results show the effectiveness of monetary policy transmission.

## Methodology

- Data: 81 regions of Russia, January 2017 – January 2025
- Method: panel data spatial models

## Relevance to Housing Market

Regional heterogeneity in monetary transmission directly affects housing markets:
- Regions with more effective transmission may see stronger cooling effects from rate hikes
- Spatial spillovers suggest that housing market dynamics in one region may affect neighbouring regions
- Subsidized lending programmes (like льготная ипотека) may weaken transmission in certain sectors/regions
